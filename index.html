HUMAN-CENTERED BIOMEDICAL ENGINEERING

Engineering human brain models to understand and improve brain health.

I am Chunhui Tian, a Ph.D. candidate at Indiana University Bloomington. My research brings together human brain organoids, microphysiological systems, neuroengineering, and computational approaches to investigate neurological diseases and neural function.

BRAIN ORGANOIDS / NEUROENGINEERING

01 / ABOUT ME

Connecting biological complexity with engineering precision.

I conduct interdisciplinary research at the intersection of organoid engineering, microfluidics, neurobiology, and neural interfaces. My work uses human-relevant experimental platforms to study how the cellular microenvironment, immune interactions, and external exposures affect brain health.

Beyond molecular and cellular measurements, I am interested in quantitative functional phenotyping: developing electrophysiological and task-based readouts that reveal how neural networks process information and respond to disease or treatment.

WHAT I WORK ON

Research directions.

Human-relevant models, mechanistic insights, and quantitative measures of neural function.

01 /

Brain organoid microphysiological systems

Engineering organoid platforms with biomimetic scaffolds and microfluidic technologies to improve physiological relevance and support controlled studies of human brain development and disease.

ORGAN-ON-CHIP · BIOMATERIALS · MICROFLUIDICS

02 /

Neuroimmune interactions & neurological disease

Using human brain organoid systems to investigate interactions between immune cells and neural tissue, including mechanisms relevant to Alzheimer's disease, aging, and neuroinflammation.

NEUROIMMUNOLOGY · DISEASE MODELING

03 /

Organoid computing & functional phenotyping

Integrating brain organoids with microelectrode arrays and computational decoding to measure neural information processing and develop richer functional readouts of network-level responses.
